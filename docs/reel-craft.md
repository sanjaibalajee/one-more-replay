# Reel craft knowledge

Reviewed 22 October 2026. Runtime guidance is versioned in `app/src/lib/replay/craft/knowledge.ts`; shared technique IDs and labels are in `techniques.ts`. These are editorial lenses for sampled evidence, not a learned ranking model or a predictor of reach.

## The useful distinction

Judge the intended job before the device. A tutorial should preserve essential instructions; a demonstration should make its result inspectable; a story may use an obstacle or delay; a montage may offer a compact mood or moment. An interview needs enough question/answer context. Fast cutting, a spoken hook and an engagement request are not universal requirements.

The app now asks the model to explain up to three techniques through four separate fields: the observed moment, an interpretation of its mechanism, a conditional tradeoff and a concrete adaptation. Empty notes are valid when evidence is thin. Findings and suggested ideas should use that reasoning without repeating the whole technique catalogue.

## Technique lenses

| Technique | Useful analysis question | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expectation reversal | What did the setup imply, and which later detail changes that reading? | Do not invent laughter or remove the context a joke needs. |
| Useful list | Does a clear criterion and distinct reasoning help someone choose? | Personal taste is not an objective product test. |
| Lead with the result | Does the opening make the task's value concrete? | Moving the reveal forward can remove a useful story question. |
| Specific viewer promise | Is the named problem or constraint fulfilled by the body? | Do not verify a price, health claim or outcome merely because it was spoken. |
| Curiosity with a payoff | What exact question remains open, and where is it resolved? | Delay alone is not retention; an undelivered promise can become bait. |
| Readable before/after | Can the same subject be compared under similar framing and conditions? | Different lighting or scale can make the comparison misleading. |
| Demonstrated proof | What can someone actually inspect beyond the creator's claim? | A testimonial or product shot does not establish causal proof. |
| Story turn | Which obstacle changes the next attempt or outcome? | A real mistake may be the distinctive part; do not automatically edit it away. |
| Useful teaching steps | Is the essential decision or quantity available where needed? | Compression can remove the information a beginner needs. |
| Words and images together | Do words explain a reason while images show an action? | Preserve accessibility captions; distinguish them from competing overlays. |
| Information progression | What new step, evidence, contrast or mood does each beat add? | Cut counts and unrecognized speech do not establish pace or dead air. |
| Motivated ending | Does the result resolve, register, or lead to one relevant action? | Do not add a sales CTA to every organic Reel or infer replays from similar frames. |

For example, a fan-making video can use an installation mistake as a story turn. The useful adaptation is to show a real constraint and the changed attempt in a different project. Copying the original price, script or accident is not the mechanism. For a tutorial, the same edit must still leave the attachment method understandable. This is an authored example, not an assessment of a saved user Reel.

## Evidence contracts

- Every technique needs actual frame or transcript citations. Captions and public counts alone cannot establish it.
- An observed before/after needs two different visual moments. A demonstrated-proof note needs a frame; spoken claims alone can only support a tentative interpretation.
- A result-first note needs opening evidence. An ending note needs a source from the final quarter. Observed progression and curiosity/payoff need multiple supplied moments.
- Words/image coordination needs both modalities at the same moment. ASR does not establish visible captions or their synchronization.
- Validation checks provenance, modalities and timing. It cannot verify semantic truth, fair conditions, the creative judgement or the claimed audience effect.
- The model cannot hear audio or observe all movement. It must not grade music, delivery, caption timing, trends, seamless loops or viewer response from incomplete evidence.

## Research basis and scope

- [YouTube's creator/product discussion on Shorts](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/) motivates early relevance and concise moments. It is creator experience and platform guidance, not evidence of a universal one-second cutoff.
- [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/creative-codes) discusses structure, visual stimulation, production and sound for ads. Organic performance remains an editorial hypothesis; sound is outside the app's current direct evidence.
- [Google's ABCDs](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551?hl=en) supports focused messaging, complementary presentation, demonstrations and objective-specific endings in advertising. The app does not transfer the published ad lift figures to competitor Reels.
- [W3C caption guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-prerecorded.html) establishes why synchronized captions carry speech and meaningful sound information. Automatic speech-only subtitles are a draft, not full caption certification.
- [YouTube retention guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en) makes first-party analytics the evidence for dips and spikes. A replay spike can also reflect confusion. The app proposes tests rather than inventing these metrics.

Knowledge is supplied to individual analysis, comparisons, adaptations, gap research and idea chat. Where a source format is known, guidance is narrowed to that format. Saved reports retain the prompt and craft revisions used when generated. No network research or extra model pass is triggered when opening a report.
